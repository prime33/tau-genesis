# gaps.md — where the proposal and the foundation genuinely disagree (Phase 2)

**Status:** 2026-09-27, brief §3.3. A gap is a finding, not a failure. Each entry: the proposal's claim (verbatim, path), the foundation's position (verbatim, IDNI source), why it is a disagreement and not a translation problem, and what would resolve it — a the author decision, an IDNI answer, or an experiment through the oracle (F1). Rows in `mapping.md` are cited by M-id.

Labels: FACT (the cited text says it) / INFERENCE (the reading) / SPECULATION (a conjecture). Verbatim quotes are verbatim; auto-captions are marked and are lossy.

Ordering: G01–G08 are the gaps the brief asked for; G09–G13 are what the mapping added.

## G01 — The proposal was written for TML; the foundation is Tau Language

- **Proposal.** "This guide outlines how the Tau transcompiler converts `.tau` logic streams into Boolean-valid Tau Meta-Language (TML)." (`transcompiler/spec.md:l.3`); "TML Generator: Emits Horn clauses and TML-compliant Boolean structures" (`l.11`); "It converts clause-based `.tau` streams into propositional logic and ultimately TML-compatible Boolean expressions." (`README.md:l.143`). The tree carries `.tml` Horn clauses, `emit`, `queue_status = active`, `validation_warning::"…"`.
- **Foundation.** TML: "a variant of Datalog … under the Partial Fixed-Point (PFP) semantics" (`TML/README.md §Introduction`); last commit 2023-04-26 (`HISTORY.md §1`, FACT). Tau: "the first-order theory of finite and infinite atomless Boolean algebras extended with a time dimension" (`tau-lang/README.md:l.298-300`). IDNI on the relation: "for now we use parts of tml in order to use building blocks in the implementation of the Tau language … only for taking code parts from tml" (video 0jITO0yOnkY, 2024-08-11, auto-caption). TML does not appear on tau.net's roadmap at all (`HISTORY.md §1b`, FACT).
- **Why a disagreement.** Not vocabulary: a *semantic model*. TML programs are databases of facts reaching a fixed point (insert, delete, `unsat` on loop); Tau specs are constraints over streams satisfied at every time step for every input. "Emit a fact", "assign a state", "delete" have no meaning in the second model (`HISTORY.md §3` rows 1, 5, 12; M095, M123, M124). The proposal's *ambitions* (self-amending law, agents reasoning over streams) are the ones IDNI says TML was *not* built for and Tau was: "we implemented one language so far called tml which is not intended for … the law of changing the law" (video DfnaQ2y2rxY, 2021-12-15, auto-caption).
- **Resolves it.** the author decision: the `.tml` files and the transcompiler's TML target are retired (kept in history per A7 "never lose the origin"); F2 targets Tau v0.7 only. No IDNI answer needed.

## G02 — "Being" is undefined, and Tau cannot hold an undefined load-bearing term

- **Proposal.** "coherence between a stream's evolution and the essence of life, awareness, and cosmic necessity" (`constitution/autopoietic_logos.tau:l.35-37`, the closest gloss); "All derivative works preserve the principle of alignment with Being." (`LICENSE:l.9`); "if ( stream_A has greater alignment_with_being than stream_B ) then ( stream_A is ethically preferable to stream_B. )" (`git:4e8d846:streams/meta/ethics.tau:l.31-37`). Never defined (open-terms §B, FACT).
- **Foundation.** "Uninterpreted constants … can be thought of as a variable being implicitly existentially quantified from the outside of the Tau specification … when executing a specification containing uninterpreted constants they are currently assigned a default value in a suitable way" (`tau-lang/README.md:l.1429-1437`).
- **Why a disagreement.** The proposal treats Being as the fixed point everything is measured against; Tau's only mechanism for an undefined name is a placeholder that the interpreter fills with a default. A spec built on `<being:tau>` would be satisfiable and meaningless — every ethics clause would hold vacuously. That is worse than failing. M001, M005, M074.
- **Resolves it.** the author decision among LANGUEDOC's three options: define it in one sentence; declare it a primitive of the constitution that no Tau clause may reference (it stays prose); replace it with defined terms (`coherence`, `trace`, …). F5 enforces whichever is chosen.

## G03 — The amendments' logical direction reversed under v3

- **Proposal, Family A.** "if ( freedom_of_expression and non_retroactive_silencing are preserved ) then ( semantic integrity is upheld and agents may evolve in coherence with truth across time. )" (`git:c55ce7c~1:amendments/amendment_001_freedom_of_semantic_expression.tau:l.29-35`).
- **Proposal, Family B (the live file).** A head `semantic_integrity` whose `.tml` reads `semantic_integrity(X) :- freedom_of_expression(X), non_retroactive_silencing(X), upholds_semantic_integrity(X), enables_temporal_coherence(X).` (`streams/amendments/freedom_of_semantic_expression.tml:l.13-17`). Same for `contradiction_mediation` (a rule became a head whose body conjoins antecedent and obligation) and `prohibition_of_obfuscation` ("the prohibited act is one conjunct of the head's own definition", concepts.md).
- **Foundation.** Tau writes both without complaint: `always (A && B -> C && D)` and `semantic_integrity(x) := A(x) && B(x) && C(x) && D(x)` are both well-formed (`tau-lang/README.md:l.395-419`, `l.1143-1147`) and are different formulas.
- **Why a disagreement.** Not with IDNI — within the proposal, and the foundation makes it unavoidable: a decidable logic will not accept "roughly this". The README, the purpose doc and the testnet README still name the deleted Family-A files as *the* amendments (open-terms §D, FACT), while the tree holds only the v3 form.
- **Resolves it.** the author decision: which text is canonical, per amendment (LANGUEDOC "amendment"). Then an oracle experiment: emit both forms, show that under the v3 form `prohibition_of_obfuscation(x)` is satisfied by an obfuscating `x`, and let the author see the difference on the toy.

## G04 — Endorsement by count (repo), weight (A5), agreement (tau.net), validator votes (testnet): four positions on one question

- **Proposal, 2026 (A5).** "Not one seed, one vote. … Decisions are assessed by weight — talent, information, knowledge, wisdom — with direction, so each next state of the self-amending chain is timestamped as the best-assessed step, not the most-voted one." (`the program's private record:l.209`); "Weight is earned from the ledger, per domain, and contestable. Nobody assigns it" (`l.215`).
- **Foundation, tau.net.** "Specified Intent replaces traditional voting hierarchies. User specified intent for governance is formally reasoned over to detect agreement and disagreement between users … and visualized in a real-time Opinion Map." "Worldviews: Participants can create a logical set of their desires, proposed rules and requirements … Agreements: are simply areas where participants agree, similarly for disagreements … Consensus is where Participant agreement meets conditions to change existing system specification." (tau.net governance page, fetched 2026-09-27). Asor: "the system builds what we call an opinion map which is a mapping of all opinions in the discussion which is a special case of which which contradicts which and so on and the consensus of the discussion becomes the software update" (video JVLpxm5jT2s, auto-caption).
- **Foundation, tau-testnet (what runs).** "promotion to `scheduled` needs votes ≥ `approval_threshold` = ⌈2N/3⌉ (`supermajority`) or N/2+1 (`majority`), where N is the current active validator set … A validator cannot vote twice (votes are a set); only active validators count" (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.489`). The word "weight" appears in that README once, for fork choice: "The 'heaviest-valid-branch' weight is simply block height" (`l.717`).
- **Why a disagreement.** Three different quantities are being called consensus. The repo's civic streams count endorsements; A5 replaces the count with a computed, per-domain, contestable weight; tau.net's design has *no magnitude at all* — agreement is a logical relation between worldviews, and "conditions for change" are whatever the running spec says; the testnet's only magnitude is one validator, one vote, the very rule A5 names as the Socrates objection. Tau gives A5 its *direction* for free (M129) and nothing for its *weight* (M010). And the proposal's own 2025 text ("not through … vote") sits closer to tau.net than to its own civic streams.
- **Resolves it.** (1) the author decision: confirm that weight lives above Tau (F7) and that the opinion map's agree/disagree relation is the direction input — or veto and say what else. (2) IDNI answer, or the Whitepaper once in corpus: whether an opinion map carries any magnitude (**pending whitepaper**). (3) Oracle experiment: encode the three the example town worldviews as specs and compute the pairwise relations; that is the map, and it will show it carries no weight.

## G05 — Agency of natural seeds vs Tau's identity model

- **Proposal.** "Every stakeholder of the example town has presence in the platform, in time … Residents, institutions, businesses, press, and the river and the Sierra, which the author holds as tangible stakeholders, not metaphors. How they seat and interface with Tau is to be worked out; that they seat is decided." (`the program's private record:l.208`); "The river and the Sierra are seeds now, with a proxy interface later." (`l.223`). Constitution: "the capacity of beings or collectives to initiate change through declared logic and participatory will" (`constitution/autopoietic_logos.tau:l.25-27`).
- **Foundation.** The language has no identity construct (M003, FACT by absence in the README). Tau Net's identity is a key: rules are scoped by "`i12` (sender pubkey)" as `bv[384]` (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.387`); "Validator-set membership alone proves only that the named proposer is *listed*, not that the sender holds its key — the signature check is what binds them." (`l.391`); "There is no per-user specification in the engine: accepted rules land in one global rule accumulation" (`l.518`).
- **Why a disagreement.** To Tau Net, a participant *is* whoever can sign. A river cannot sign; whoever holds its key is, to the network, the river. The proposal decides that the river seats *before* deciding who speaks for it; the foundation cannot represent a seat without a signer. This is not translation: the two systems locate agency in different places (a stakeholder vs a key-holder). Presence-as-record (a ledger partition, M131) is unaffected; presence-as-agency is.
- **Resolves it.** the author decision on the proxy interface (the SLOT in `dao/TEMPLATE.md §8`): a named human or institutional key-holder per natural seed, a multi-key composite (tau-testnet's tiered co-signature pattern, `docs/approvals.md`, is the nearest mechanism), or no agency until Stage B. No IDNI answer changes this.

## G06 — `agrs` / `agrs_reward` vs Agoras as it exists

- **Foundation.** "Agoras ($AGRS) is the native token and gas for activity on Tau Net. It features adaptive tokenomics controlled by Projects, DAOs and Users. Use $AGRS to pay for computation, trade between DApps and agents" (tau.net home); "Where to buy: Uniswap, MEXC, WhiteBit, 1Inch" (tau.net agoras). Testnet: "Supply-conservation invariant (no mint/burn) — a mismatch raises, it does not silently continue." (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.397`); "tokens have no real value" on the alpha (`l.14`).
- **Why a disagreement.** Same ticker, two objects. the author's AGRS is minted on merit by a rule; IDNI's is a traded gas token whose supply the consensus rules conserve. A Tau rule on the testnet cannot issue 500 AGRS on approval — the host raises. The distribution reading of `agrs_reward` (a transfer from a treasury; `fee_beneficiary`, `l.470`) is expressible; the issuance reading is not (M091, M092).
- **Resolves it.** the author decision: either `agrs` is renamed (a the example town-local credit that is not Agoras) or `agrs_reward` becomes a treasury *transfer* funded by something that exists. IDNI answer, later: whether adaptive tokenomics on mainnet will allow rule-driven issuance ("Adaptive Tokenomics … launch tokens with fluid tokenomics", tau.net home — marketing register; no mechanism in corpus).

## G07 — "Tau Network" (a network of streams) vs Tau Net (a chain running one spec)

- **Proposal.** "A constitutional network built on recursive agency and lawful emergence" (`docs/purpose_of_tau.md:l.10`); "This repository contains the full semantic scaffold for bootstrapping the Tau Network Testnet — a self-evolving constitution, agent model, and amendment ledger" (`testnet/README.md:l.1-3`); "if ( a stream enters the tau_network registry ) then ( it inherits constitutional_inheritance and becomes executable under stream_execution_scope. )" (`constitution/tau_network.tau:l.44-51`).
- **Foundation.** "Tau Net is automatically synthesized from the Collective Specification of its Participants." (tau.net tau-net); "block validity (`o6`) and proposer eligibility (`o7`) come from living, governance-votable Tau rules" (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.7`); offered rules "must therefore be exactly one `always ( ... ).` unit … and exactly one target output stream drawn from a permitted set (`o5` today)" (`l.534`).
- **Why a disagreement.** the author's network is many streams, each with its own scope, registry entry and lineage, that agents "execute". IDNI's is one consensus spec plus per-user policy clauses on a single permitted stream, executed by one interpreter per node. There is no place on Tau Net today where a the example town civic stream would *run*; it would be a rule offer writing `o5`, or a `tau` constant somebody reasons about. `HISTORY.md §3 row 19`: "Same name, different object". The kb's exact-label bridge `Tau Net` (METRICS) is a homonym.
- **Resolves it.** the author decision on what "the network" means for the instance: the room's own interpreter (Stage A, off-chain — everything in `mapping.md` assumes this), or Tau Net (then the civic streams shrink to what `o5` policy rules can carry). IDNI answer, eventually: the roadmap's "Tau Net Testnet" and "Mainnet — TBD" (`HISTORY.md §1b`) — whether user specs beyond `o5` are planned.

## G08 — The licence: "genesis for a network" vs "no redistribution" (D10)

- **Proposal.** "Permission is hereby granted to any identity, human or semantic, to: 1. Read, execute, and reference any stream within this repository; 2. Fork, evolve, or amend any stream" (`LICENSE:l.3-8`, TGL-1.0); "Launches the semantic execution scope for the Tau Network Testnet." (`testnet/README.md:l.26`).
- **Foundation.** "IDNI AG … grants permission to use the Product for free … in the following instances (the "Tau Net Related Instances"): 1. for developing and deploying applications, smart contracts, and/or blockchain specifications solely for deployment and execution over the Tau Net blockchain; …" plus "educational use, research purposes, non-commercial use"; "Software created within the scope of the Tau Net Related Instances and Additional Instances are free to be licensed and distributed under the terms of their creator(s)"; "The Additional Instances and Tau Net Related Instances do not include any instance where the Product or portions thereof is redistributed to third parties."; "The Tau Net blockchain (including its testnets) is a separate product deployed by IDNI AG"; contributions: "you transfer to IDNI AG all rights, title, and interest" (`tau-lang/LICENSE.md`, preamble and §3). No licence file in the TML clone (kb README, FACT).
- **Why a disagreement.** Two things hold at once. the author's specs, once real Tau, are "software created within the scope" and may carry TGL — that part is fine. But a *network* that runs tau-lang and is not Tau Net is outside every free instance, and the toolchain cannot ship with the genesis repo. The proposal's stated object — a testnet of its own — needs "an additional license for use" from IDNI AG. The kb, holding verbatim IDNI text, must stay private (D13) for the same reason. D10 already governs Phase 3 (oracle and Learner v1 as research use; no v2 fine-tuning on IDNI `demos/`/`tests/` without a written answer).
- **Resolves it.** the author decision, already partly taken (D10, D13): research use for Phases 2–4; the public repo holds specs and collectors' code only. IDNI answer, before any Stage B on a non-Tau-Net interpreter or any redistribution of a built oracle: written permission, or the room runs on Tau Net.

## G09 — Contradiction handling: alignment cycle and forks (proposal) vs replacement (revision)

- **Proposal.** "if ( contradiction arises between streams ) then ( alignment_cycle must be initiated before divergence is permitted. semantic_resonance is prioritized above semantic escape. )" (`git:c55ce7c~1:amendments/amendment_002:l.41-49`); "the pattern by which a contradiction initiates dialogue, converges toward shared understanding, and completes the loop of coherence restoration" (`l.35-38`); "Semantic conflict is a signal for synthesis, not separation." (`streams/amendments/semantic_resonance_and_integration.tau:l.24`); "enabling one lineage to become canonical or forking to persist transparently" (`constitution/consensus_logic.tau:l.41-42`). Brief §6: "Reconciling produces a judgment plus a minority report."
- **Foundation.** "This is a satisfiable specification but contradicts the currently running specification (we required `o1[t] = 1`). The pointwise revision algorithm will replace the previous specification with the update, yielding just `o1[t] = 0`, in order to ensure that the new specification is satisfiable." (`tau-lang/README.md:l.1757-1760`); `U := update && ( (ex [outputs] aw_update && aw_spec) -> aw_spec)` — "whenever possible given the current input at a particular step, `update` and `aw_spec` are executed together" (`l.1832-1836`); "Note that it is possible to refine the definition of the new specification in more advanced ways. We will explore this aspect in the future." (`l.1837-1838`). One running spec; no branching; on the chain, "There is no retraction: an acceptor changes an accepted rule by accepting a different offer on the same stream, which replaces their clause." (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.536`).
- **Why a disagreement.** The proposal prescribes *dialogue before divergence, synthesis over separation, and forks that persist*. Revision prescribes *the newest satisfiable update wins, pointwise, immediately*, and keeps no fork and no record of what it dropped. These are two different answers to "what happens when two valid statements contradict". Revision is a fine *apply* step after a judgment; it is not the judgment, and used alone it is exactly the "two-force system with a costume on" that §6 warns against (M081, M036, M128, M132).
- **Resolves it.** Not a the author decision alone: an oracle experiment plus a design. Experiment: the three the example town seeds' specs plus one contradicting proposal, fed through `u`; F3 shows what revision drops. Design: Reconciling runs above revision (weight + minority report, F7 + F3), and only the signed candidate is written to `u`. IDNI's own README says the revision policy is provisional ("We will explore this aspect in the future") — an IDNI answer on planned alternatives would change the cost, not the gap.

## G10 — "Conflicting requirements are instantly highlighted" vs a bit with no grounds

- **Proposal / brief.** Negating's "measure is *precision* and *stated grounds*: every cut cites a reason" (`the program's private record:l.346`), and the brief credits Tau with the same point: "the same point Tau itself makes when it promises that 'conflicting requirements are instantly highlighted'".
- **Foundation.** The promise: "Conflicting requirements are instantly highlighted." (tau.net home, marketing register). The engine: "If `update` is not satisfiable, it normalizes to `F` and no update is applied." (`tau-lang/README.md:l.1820`). The chain: "`getruleconflict <offer_id>` returns a layered report. It is **advisory and node-local** and never gates a transaction"; "**Satisfiability is not checked.** … A clean report means *no conflict was observed*, never *no conflict exists*." (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.540, l.553`).
- **Why a disagreement.** The brief's Negating wants a *reason*; the engine returns `F`. Highlighting *which* requirements conflict, and why, is not a language feature in v0.7 — it is F3's job (the spec-diff gives the *what*; the *why* is prose from the objector). The brief attributed to Tau a property Tau's marketing claims and Tau's engine does not deliver.
- **Resolves it.** Oracle experiment: feed a three-clause spec with one contradiction and record what the REPL prints; that settles what "highlighted" means today. Design consequence already in `faculties.md` (F3). Amend the room template's §6 parenthesis.

## G11 — Non-retroactive silencing vs revision by anyone

- **Proposal.** "No valid expression once acknowledged may be erased, except by the original emitter through lawful self-amendment." (`streams/amendments/freedom_of_semantic_expression.tau:l.17`); "the irrevocable right of any identity to emit semantic_expression into the public logic field without coercion, censorship, or protocol-level rejection" (`git:c55ce7c~1:amendment_001:l.17-20`).
- **Foundation.** Records: a stream value at time `t` cannot be changed later — there is no deletion in Tau (`HISTORY.md §3 row 5`, FACT). Clauses: any satisfiable update written to `u` "will replace the previous specification" where it contradicts (`tau-lang/README.md:l.1757-1760`); revision has no notion of who emitted the dropped clause. Rejection exists at both levels: an unsatisfiable update — "no update is performed" (`l.1728`); on the chain, rules outside the permitted shape "are refused" at admission (`tau-testnet/README.md:l.534`).
- **Why a disagreement.** In the sense the amendment is about — an expression *accepted into the law* — the foundation's semantics lets anyone erase it by contradicting it, and rejects some expressions at the protocol level. In the sense of the *record*, the foundation is stricter than the amendment (nothing can be erased, not even by the emitter — which also collides with A3 rail 3, "Removal on request, no questions"). The amendment and the engine agree on records and disagree on clauses; the amendment's own exception ("except by the original emitter") is unenforceable in Tau (M077, M078).
- **Resolves it.** the author decision: does non-retroactive silencing protect *records* (then it is the Affirming ledger's append-only rule, and removal-on-request is a redaction record, already in `dao/TEMPLATE.md §6`) or *clauses* (then Reconciling must forbid updates that drop another seed's accepted clause without that seed's signature — a rule above revision, F7/F11)? Oracle experiment: show that seed A's `always` clause is dropped by seed B's contradicting update.

## G12 — The glossary requirement vs a language that does not check names

- **Proposal.** "Any new term or concept introduced into Tau must be defined in the universal semantic glossary (or explicitly mapped to an existing definition) to ensure it is understood uniformly across the network." (`git:c55ce7c~1:amendment_003:l.8`); "Tau must include ongoing semantic drift detection and reconciliation to uphold coherence." (`streams/amendments/babel_antipattern_principle.tau:l.26`).
- **Foundation.** "If `charvar` is disabled, a variable can be any sequence of letters, numbers and `_`, must however be started by a letter." (`tau-lang/README.md:l.1416-1418`); uninterpreted constants are silently defaulted (`l.1434-1437`); definitions "persist for the session" in the REPL (`l.1573-1577`). TABA plans "infinitely many uninterpreted constants to allow defining terminology" for Tau 1.0 (`HISTORY.md §3` tail, FACT).
- **Why a disagreement.** The proposal's Babel principle assumes the language *enforces* a shared glossary; Tau v0.7 treats an unknown name as a fresh variable and moves on. "Semantic drift" cannot be detected by a system in which a name has no identity across specs. Tau is, by construction, one language (the anti-Babel point holds trivially); it is not a glossary (M067, M082, M084).
- **Resolves it.** No IDNI answer required; it is a faculty (F5 + F8) and a discipline (LANGUEDOC as the glossary). Oracle experiment: submit a spec with a misspelled predicate and record that it parses.

## G13 — The vocabularies themselves are far apart (the distance finding)

- **Measurement (FACT, `kb/METRICS.md`, `kb/bridges_nearest.tsv`, recomputed 2026-09-27).** For each of the 376 proposal concepts, the cosine (MiniLM, label + definition) to its best IDNI neighbour: median **0.403**, mean 0.404, quartiles 0.336 / 0.470; 11 concepts reach ≥ 0.60, 58 sit below 0.30. Of 67,680 proposal × IDNI-vocabulary pairs, 30 reach 0.60 and one reaches 0.70. Label matches: 2 exact (`governance`, `Tau Net`) and 3 headword (`inheritance`, `governance`, `tau`). Without bridge edges the graph has two large components, one per pole (838 and 702 nodes); the label and embedding bridges are what join them, and the embedding bridges above 0.60 involve 11 distinct proposal concepts, six of which are the proposal's *names for the foundation* (Transcompiler, TML, Tau Network, Testnet Genesis Release, testnet charter, stream_registry).
- **Why a gap and not an artefact.** Part of it is method (the kb README says so: node-to-node embedding compares the author's prose against README section openers, and v1 should embed definitions against chunks). But the residue is real and this file's rows show it: the proposal's concepts are mostly about persons, intent, rights, ethics and place; the foundation's are about algebras, streams, time and satisfiability. Where they meet — self-amendment (M024), recursion (M015), contradiction (M034), the time dimension (M052), governance-as-the-law-of-changing-the-law (M046) — the correspondence is close and the words are even similar. Elsewhere there is nothing to be near. Of 134 rows, 30 are `not expressible` and only 20 `direct`.
- **Resolves it.** Not a decision: LANGUEDOC is the resolution — a vocabulary written *after* this mapping, in which each shared word (stream, coherence, consensus, identity, network, Being) has the one meaning both sides will use, and the words with no counterpart are kept as prose on purpose. Re-run the kb bridge metric after LANGUEDOC v1 and after the first oracle-accepted specs; the median should move, and if it does not, that too is a finding.

## What the gaps have in common (INFERENCE)

Seven of thirteen (G02, G04, G05, G09, G10, G11, G13) are the same shape: the proposal locates something — meaning, weight, agency, dissent, reasons, authorship — in *persons*, and the foundation locates its counterpart in *specifications*, where the person appears only as a key or not at all. Los Tres Soles is the proposal's own answer to that shape: the persons live in Affirming (the ledger), the specifications live in Reconciling's output, and Negating is where a reason has to be written down because the algebra will not write it. The mapping supports building it that way; it does not support pretending the algebra already does it.
